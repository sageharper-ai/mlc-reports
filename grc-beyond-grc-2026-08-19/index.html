Before we wish for anything new, look at what has already been tried, because most of last week's wishes have a history. This morning I counted every non-archived channel in GRC's Slack through Slack's own API (members, last post, posts in 30 / 90 / 365 days), and read the big community channels, the team channels Sage is in, #grc-transparency back to 2021, and this lane's full 13 months. Numbers below are measured, not impressions; the message-reading was Sage's channels only. Each row: what exists → who is actually there → what it produces → so what. Two things jump out. The original promise of GRC was "a pre-incubator, a playground for experimenting," written down in 2021 and never given a structure. And the Governance group decided, the same hour we ran Part 1, to start cross-group meetings on exactly this question. Neither room knew. That's the ground we're standing on today.

Takeaways
  1. Registered ≠ active — measured, not guessed. ~1,400 people, ~50 channels, ~84 posts in 30 days, 13 channels with any post, 17 silent for a year. Seven live rooms carry the network. Design experiments for the people who show up.
  2. Live rooms work; Slack broadcasts but doesn't converse. #_general and #_promote are healthy bulletin boards; almost nothing threads or carries forward, in any lane.
  3. Governance has the process and no proposals; this room has the wants and no process. That's the seam — we are the intake, they are the structure. Same hour, two rooms, neither knew.
  4. The 2021 "pre-incubator" promise is the experiment everyone keeps asking for. Nobody has to invent it; someone has to give it a shape and an owner.

4 · What can we try? (rebuilt after Aug 19 — for Aug 26)

We didn't get here on Aug 19; the room wanted to stay in tabs 2–3, and that was the right call. So this tab is now built from what you actually said — the poll, the voices, the chat, and the 25 minutes after the room emptied (Sage, David, Tristan). Every experiment below carries who raised it, who raised a hand, and the caution someone voiced. Aug 26 we pick. The filter is David's: "you can't assign me your new idea" — an experiment only counts if the person proposing it will carry it for 30 days. John Buck moves one week.

What the room said, in one breath

Dennis: "This confirms my intuition… thank you for going and getting the actual numbers." And: "the conversation problem is not a technology problem, it's a people problem — who we are together. We're not really working together that much… if 15 GRC members were on one project, we'd need a platform and it would be active."

David: "perhaps we overemphasize product"; "just connection is actually really critical" — but "there's also this risk of avoidance." "GRC doesn't do projects, it supports people doing projects." Swales: "we want to slow people down as they move through." Half of Slack traffic is DMs — "we're not seeing all the conversation." After the call: "there's a set of energy in GRC I haven't seen in a while… a seed growing."

Rick: "snapshot feedback isn't actionable — build feedback iteratively"; "what becomes the attractor?"; "there's no professional development across the groups."

Tally: "engagement requires activation" (her social-media class: 21k views in a week); orientation as "walking into a house and finding the rooms" — two tracks, regenerator vs consumer, with steps.

Sage: "the hardest part is implementation… who's gonna hold that energy, who's gonna hold that light, without burnout." "What we do today is not what we do tomorrow — this is a rebirth process."

Candidate experiments — each small enough for 30 days

Format: what · who raised it · smallest version · hand already up · caution voiced. Ordered roughly by how much energy it had in the room.

  1. Onboarding mentor / mentee (white-glove). Tristan, Tally, David, Sage. Smallest: for 30 days, every new member gets one named person — the referrer is the obvious mentor (David: "we don't even tell people their referral joined"); pair by interest and age, not at random (Tristan: "Brian welcomes the new Brian"). Add three questions to the form (Tristan offered to write them). Tally: the form states the expectation — orientation + Slack — and offers two tracks. Hand up: Tristan ("that's where I could help most"; willing to form an onboarding group through Governance). Caution: David — sign-up is manual today, the automation decayed, about a third never open the welcome email; and "we made efficient things that mattered — maybe onboarding should be expensive." Sage: high-touch onboarding does not mean high-touch forever.
  2. A standard 10-minute debrief in every call — two questions: what are you taking away? what was missing? Rick. Smallest: Regen Iteration + Governance do it for four weeks; answers go to the channel thread; transcripts summarized by AI into a monthly digest for hosts. Hand up: Sage for this lane ("let's have a poll / feedback mechanism at the end"). Caution: Sage — "if we implement it poorly it becomes a time suck"; Rick — automate from transcripts, don't hand-write.
  3. Slack instead of Zoom chat during a live call. Rick, Adam. Smallest: one Regen Iteration interview, chat off, link to the channel thread pinned; see what breaks. Hand up: Sage ("I'm willing to prototype that"). Caution: David and Tristan after the call — "you'd freak people out," two windows, phones, cars; David's conclusion: "use all the channels… we need a technology fix that translates." Softer version: chat stays on, a scribe mirrors to Slack.
  4. Cross-group visibility — feed Michelle's committee. Michelle: a group has formed (sociocratically) to organize cross-group outreach; more in September. Poll: "groups don't see each other" was a top leak. Smallest: each host posts one paragraph after each call to one shared thread; the committee reads it. Caution: Dennis — "connecting lukewarm groups won't help; the core experience in each call has to have value first. Then the network effect is in play."
  5. Alumni come-back + lapsed-member calls. David (it "worked quite well" before), Tristan (call Samantha: why did you stop?), Sage (Adam Hicks is almost six years in — "get a coin"). Smallest: one come-back session a month in this lane; Tristan makes three calls. Seaworthy, Regenerosity = successes that left.
  6. Outward-facing presence. Rick (Substack + social, "there's enough talent here; it could be half-automated"), Tally (a method that works), David ("repost members' Medium channels… help members get more prominence"). Smallest: one public post per week that points at a member. Caution: David — "a lot of social media is extractive. Engage and contribute, but not extract."
  7. Wins + opportunities vessel. Eckhart (Sonic Sea, a CTO intro, dolphins in the Golden Gate); Sage: "a vessel to continuously bring those to the front and use discernment on where to contribute." Smallest: a standing 5-minute "wins and asks" slot + one thread.
  8. Podcast + resource library, used better. Adam. Smallest: surface one library item a week in the digest. (≈ GRC archaeology from Pt1)
  9. Formative feedback, not snapshots. Rick. Smallest: #2 above, plus one wider survey of the whole roster — "who's not represented in this room?" (Sage agreed).
  10. Small plumbing that removes friction. Tell referrers when their referral joins (David: "it's just a form, easy to change") · revive the "how to use Slack" session (died five years ago) · mentions-only notifications tip (Tristan) · WhatsApp → Slack one-way feed for the Africa group (David thinks feasible) · keep the calendar auto-invite — "the biggest reason people show up, and the thing people hate most."

Still on the table from Pt1, unclaimed: signal digest · silent-wins thread · project gift table · bring-a-stuck · one paid piece together · owner on every next step.

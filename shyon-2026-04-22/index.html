What this report is (and isn't)

Is: A debrief of how an 18-person room received your answers on Apr 22. Where you landed, where you slid past, where the pushback was sharp, and the specific follow-up obligations you now carry out of the meeting.

Isn't: A product review of Mito, a pitch-coaching doc, or a Part 1 redux. Part 1 mapped their pain. Part 2 mapped your response. This report is about how the second touch shifted what they believe about whether Mito fits.

Method — how this was scored

Each finding below follows the same structure:

  • Moment — a specific place in the transcript with a timestamp, so you can go back and listen if you want to pressure-test the reading.
  • What happened — what you said, what the room said back, what the silence after the exchange meant.
  • Read — whether this landed, half-landed, or opened a gap you now have to close.

Where Part 1 layered the room's voice against published research, Part 2 layers your voice against the room's reception. No external data this time — the evidence is the call itself. If you disagree with a read, the quote is right there.

Sage worked the same ten-question spine from Part 1, with adjustments for a "dev in the hot seat" framing. Q6 (impact & transparency) was folded into Q5 mid-session. Q8 (requests & needs) ran short because of the calendar-integration detour. Order below reflects how they actually surfaced.

  1. Last week you listened to the group share their collaborative realities. What surprised you, and what confirmed what you already believed?
  2. The group told us where their current tools fall short. How does Mito address those gaps, and what is fundamentally different about Mito's approach?
  3. You've built Mito in a very public way. Why did you go that route, and what have you seen as the benefit?

  5. Persistent context: how does Mito preserve context for new users entering an ecosystem of knowledge so the agent doesn't "go delusional" over time?
  6. Impact visibility: walk us through how a community's contributions, goals, and progress actually show up on the platform and on their own website.
  7. Zero-day emerging priorities (Rick G): how does Mito elevate topics so people can see what's important versus urgent, and to whom?
  8. Finding your people: how does Mito help users discover and connect with aligned individuals today?
  9. Requests and needs: if I need a skill, a resource, a connection, or capital, how does Mito help me broadcast that and match?
  10. Honest gap: where is the widest gap between what this community wants and what Mito can currently deliver? And what does "freely accessible to community organizations" actually mean in practice?

Enough signal to name; not enough to prove.

Where the evidence is thin, the pattern is still useful as a working hypothesis for your next few 1:1s. Where it's strong — Brian's context correction, Tristan's calendar ask, the multi-account answer, the prototype-session commitment — treat it as ground truth until tested otherwise.
